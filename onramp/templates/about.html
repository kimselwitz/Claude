Why it works this way

The actual problem

The hard part of texting isn't writing. It's the gap between seeing a message and answering it. Every hour in that gap raises the price of the reply — now it needs an apology, and the apology needs an explanation — which widens the gap. Left alone it ends in a thread nobody can face, and that's how people lose friends they like.

So the app only has four moves

Read without owing. Deciding "this needs no reply" is one tap, and it's a real answer. Most messages are information, not requests.

Buy time in one tap. A holding reply costs a second and does most of the work — it converts "they're ignoring me" into "they'll get to it," which is the part that was actually frightening.

Never start from blank. Replies get assembled from sentences you've already approved. The blank box is where the paralysis lives.

Cheapest first. Urgency-sorting puts the worst message on top, which is where avoidance starts. Momentum gets you to the hard one; dread doesn't.

And some things it refuses to do

No red. No badge with a number in it — a count you can see from every screen is a debt tally. No streaks, because a broken streak on a bad week is a reason to delete the app. It never says overdue. Nothing auto-sends. Every screen has an exit that isn't "reply now."

Built for demand avoidance as much as anxiety: an app that tells you to reply is an app that makes replying harder.
